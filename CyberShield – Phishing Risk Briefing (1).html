    Anatomy of a scam

    Four tricks packed into one short text.

    URGENT!1 Your SBI bank account will be blocked2 today. Verify your KYC3 immediately: sbi-kyc-update.xyz4
    1. Panic by deadline. Urgency leaves no time to check.
    2. Fear of loss. Threatening to block an account makes people act first.
    3. A request real banks avoid. KYC updates are not done through links in texts.
    4. A fake address. It borrows the SBI name but is not sbi.co.in, and ends in an uncommon .xyz.

    If you already clicked or replied

    1. Stop. Do not enter any more details or codes.
    2. Call your bank on the number printed on your card. Block the card or UPI ID if money moved.
    3. Change the password of any account you typed in, and any other where you reuse it.
    4. Report it on cybercrime.gov.in or call the National Cybercrime Helpline 1930 quickly; early reports improve the chance of freezing money.
    5. Keep screenshots of the message, link and payment details.

    Questions

     

    Is my message uploaded anywhere?

    No. The analysis runs inside your browser. Recent scans are stored only on your own device.

    Why does it say "Low risk" instead of "Safe"?

    No tool can prove a message is harmless. A low score means no common warning signs were found, so confirm anything important through the official app.

    Can it be wrong?

    Yes. It scores wording and link structure with fixed rules, so a carefully written scam can score low and an odd but genuine message can score higher. Use it as a second opinion.

    CyberShield is a screening aid that scores wording and link structure with weighted rules. It cannot guarantee a message is safe; when unsure, open the official app or website yourself.
